Flare — Daily Cheat Card

v2 · Sep 4 2026 · supersedes v1 (Jul 2026, extension bias) · TFESI L4–L5 on Sep 14
Direction changed since v1. The Jul 28 MRI put the problem in the lateral recess, not the foramen — and flexion opens the recess. Loaded extension is retired — prone press-ups stay, standing extensions go. If this card and anything older disagree, this card is right.

Morning — the worst window, 5–6am

  • Dead hangs 30–60s × 2–3 — decompress first. Step down off the bar.
  • Prone lying 3–5 min — pillow under chest.
  • Prone press-ups ×10, 1–2 sets — hips stay down. Unloaded extension only.
  • Get moving. Position change is the intervention, not any one posture.
  • No forward-bend work. Never within two hours of waking — disc bending stress is highest then.
  • Stand up in stages off the floor: prone → hands and knees → half-kneel → stand.

Canal walk — daily

  • 2 miles. First mile easy, forward.
  • Backward intervals — 2–3 poles work / 1 pole rest. Maintained base, not a progression.
  • 30s forward-gait reset after each interval.
  • Short steps, quick cadence, land under the hip. Don't reach out front.

Through the day

  • Break sustained positions every 45–60 min with 90s of walking.
  • Tingling climbing → get up and walk. Don't hold a shape.
  • Fed and hydrated before any session. No fasted training.
  • Position and pacing cycling is normal flare behaviour — not a failure.

Afternoon window — 11am–4pm

  • This is where loading goes. Training at 5pm is training into the wall.
  • Flexion rung — current rung only, 5 breaths × 3. Log the landmark.
  • One plane drill — rotation, lateral, or deep squat sit.
  • The bend is a measure, not a stretch. Record it like a number.

Evening

  • Prone press-ups ×8–10, reduced ROM.
  • Diaphragmatic breathing, prone ×10 — 4 in / hold 2 / 6 out. This genuinely helped on Jul 24.
  • Deep squat sit, supported 30–90s — only if the day was green.
  • Icing if it's spiking. Also earned its place in July.
  • Transition straight to sleep position.

Traffic light — governs every session

●Green — leg same or reduced → continue at the current rung.
●Yellow — back up, leg unchanged → fine, log it. Symptoms moving toward the spine is the direction you want. Judge by direction, not intensity.
●Red — leg pain increases → stop. Drop one rung. Note the trigger; don't repeat that variable tomorrow.

On a bad day — what changes, and what doesn't

  • Drop a rung, don't drop the ladder. Graded exposure survives a flare by reducing, not by stopping. A zero day costs more than a reduced one.
  • Baseline is not zero. Constant background radiculopathy is the floor this card measures from — its presence is not a red light. Only climbing is.
  • Hurt is not harm. A flare is a flare. The Jul 28 imaging is mild — "possible abutment," no compression, no stenosis. Symptoms rising does not mean the disc moved.
  • Morning score is a bad gauge on its own. Judge the session at 24 and 48 hours.
The 48-hour rule — the one that actually matters. Back to baseline by next morning → banked, repeat it. Elevated at 24h but back by 48h → that's your ceiling, hold the rung. Still up at 48h → drop one. And: two sessions in a row needing 48 hours to settle is a stop, however good the days in between felt. Early June gave that signal twice and it got ridden through. The third one was the big flare.

After the injection — Sep 14, TFESI L4–L5

  • First 48–72 hours: soreness is the needle, not the outcome. Common, expected, says nothing about whether it worked. Walking only. Don't log it as a rung response.
  • Onset typically 2–7 days. Peak 2–6 weeks.
  • The steroid quiets the alarm, not the anatomy. The recess is as narrow on Sep 21 as on Sep 13. Advance at the same rate as before — the window buys you climbing without the pain tax, not skipped rungs.
  • Read the 48-hour column through the window. The 24-hour signal is chemically suppressed — that's the point of the injection.
  • Resume at the rung held on Sep 13. Not one above it.

Prone extension stays · standing extension goes — not a contradiction

  • They are different exercises wearing the same word. Prone press-ups are unloaded: hips down, pelvis carried by the floor, lower body passive, the segment extends without bearing bodyweight. Standing extensions are loaded and active — the spine holds bodyweight while the whole chain guards.
  • Plausible mechanism for why one helps and the other doesn't: unloaded prone extension can migrate disc material forward, easing the dorsal bulge that forms the front wall of the recess. Flexion opens the recess from behind — flavum tightens, facets distract. Two routes to the same relief. Loaded standing extension takes neither: it narrows the recess without the unloading that lets the disc respond.
  • So: prone yes, standing no. Nothing here is inconsistent — the variable that matters is load, not direction.

Medication — hard rules

Maintaining costs less than chasing. · Any pre-syncopal symptom — vision tunnelling, greying, unusual lightheadedness — ends the session. Not "sit down and continue." Retired from v1 — don't reach for these

Standing extensions ×10 every 1–2 hrs · Tingling → 10 standing extensions · NexWave TENS at 4pm (suspended Jul 20, electrode contact sensitivity) · "No flexion · no core/stabilization · no hip strengthening" — that Phase-1 prohibition is reversed: flexion is the direction, and Pallof, glute med and BFR are all live. · Lidocaine patches (no effect over ~7 days).

The one rule: leg pain climbing = back off a rung. Pain concentrating toward the spine = progress. When in doubt, protect the leg — but reduce, don't stop.
