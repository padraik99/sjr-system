## Tier 1 — directly on your problem

### Annina Schmid — Professor of Pain Neurosciences, University of Oxford
**The single most relevant researcher to your exact situation.** Nuffield Department of
Clinical Neurosciences. Her programme is nerve mechanosensitivity, entrapment
neuropathies, and — critically for you — *distinguishing mechanical nerve compromise from
neuroinflammatory and central sensitization mechanisms in the same patient.* That is
precisely the "possible abutment, but also allodynia" problem you've been living inside.

Start here: **"Entrapment neuropathies: a contemporary approach to pathophysiology,
clinical assessment, and management"** (PAIN Reports, 2020) — open access.

She also works on neurodynamics with actual mechanistic grounding, which is the honest
version of the nerve-gliding material floating around elsewhere.

### The Curtin group — Wernli, Kent, Caneiro, O'Sullivan
**This is the forward-bend answer you were looking for**, and it turns out to be a
sustained research programme rather than an opinion. Three consecutive JOSPT papers:

- *How Movement Is Assessed Matters. Changes in Forward Bending During Cognitive
  Functional Therapy* (JOSPT 2023)
- *Improvements in Forward Bending Are Related to Improvements in Pain and Disability
  During CFT* (JOSPT 2024)
- *Relationships Between Changes in Forward Bending, Pain Catastrophizing, and Pain
  Self-Efficacy During CFT* (JOSPT 2025)

The through-line: restoring forward bending isn't merely *safe*, it tracks with
improvement in pain and disability. Your instinct that never regaining full forward bend
was a real gap is supported by a dedicated line of research, not just plausible.

Underneath it sits **Cognitive Functional Therapy** and the **RESTORE trial**
(Lancet 2023, n≈492, three arms) with **3-year follow-up published 2025** in Lancet
Rheumatology — one of the strongest results in the chronic low back pain literature, and
it held at three years, which most things don't.

Peter O'Sullivan is the senior figure; JP Caneiro is the one to read on movement-related
fear and bending specifically.

### Kika Konstantinou — Keele University
Sciatica in primary care: prognosis and who actually needs what.
- **ATLAS cohort** — prognosis of sciatica and back-related leg pain (Spine Journal 2017)
- **SCOPiC RCT** — stratified vs usual care for sciatica (Lancet Rheumatology 2020)

Useful for calibrating expectations about trajectory, and for the question of whether
escalation is warranted at any given point. Relevant to how you frame the Sept 8 visit.

## Tier 2 — framework, and the tension you're living

### Greg Lehman — physiotherapist and chiropractor
**"Reconciling Biomechanics with Pain Science."** Free pain-science workbooks on his site.
He is the explicit bridge between the McGill-style "protect the spine from flexion" model
already in your library and the graded-exposure model above. You are currently standing
between those two positions with one foot on each — Lehman is the person who has done the
work of reconciling them rather than picking a side. Read him before you try to hold both.

### Chris Maher — Institute for Musculoskeletal Health, University of Sydney
Trials and guidelines. Senior author on the pregabalin sciatica trial (NEJM 2017) already
in the protocol. Good for the "does this drug/intervention actually do anything" question,
which keeps coming up in your care.

## Already in your library — worth re-reading against the new diagnosis

**Stuart McGill.** The flexion-intolerance model, the Big Three. Not wrong, but it was
built for a different question than the one you now have, and the July MRI changed which
compartment is involved. Read him as the counterweight to Tier 1, not as the settled
answer — and note that his framework is where "never bend forward" advice usually
originates, which is the belief the Curtin work is actively testing.

---

## Read in this order

1. Schmid, *Entrapment neuropathies* — mechanism, and where sensitization fits
2. Wernli et al. 2024, forward bending ↔ pain and disability — the case for the ladder
3. Lehman workbooks — how to hold McGill and CFT at the same time
4. Konstantinou, ATLAS — trajectory and expectations
5. RESTORE 3-year follow-up — does any of this last
