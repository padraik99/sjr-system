THIS ISN'T A PROGRAM. IT'S PERMISSION.

Berkeley will hand you the real training — the sprints, the lifts, the load. This is the other thing. Movement for its own sake: keep the foot awake, the body supple, refresh between hard days and long travel. Pick what feels good today, skip the rest. Nothing here is a rep you owe. It's a menu, not a plan — the way Ido Portal, Katy Bowman, and Kelly Starrett treat movement as something you get to do, not something you grind.

The one line that keeps it safe

Your navicular is healed enough to walk, not healed enough to test. It's a watershed bone — it earns its trust back slowly. So on your own: no impact, no explosive foot work, nothing that makes the old spot speak up. The pounding belongs to the track staff and your PT, who can watch it. If a movement here provokes that navicular spot, it's not for today — back off and tell your PT. When in doubt, ask them before adding load.
Foot revival
The star of this phase. Seven weeks in a boot makes a foot forget how to be a foot — these wake it back up. Barefoot, on a soft floor, unhurried.
Toe yoga1–2 min
Big toe down while the others lift, then flip it. Clumsy at first — that's the point. You're re-teaching the wiring.

Movement snacks

The 6-minute refresh
A tiny sequence for a stiff morning, a travel day, or after too much sitting. Tomorrow's flight counts. No warm-up needed — this is the warm-up.
  1. Ankle CARs — 3 each way, both feet
  2. Toe yoga + toe splay — a slow minute
  3. Deep squat sit — settle in, breathe, 30–60s
  4. Ground transitions — up and down 3–4 ways
  5. Easy hang — one or two, decompress
  6. Spinal waves — cat-cow into a gentle twist

How to use this

  • It's a menu, not a plan. One item or the whole thing. No streak to keep, no guilt for skipping.
  • It complements the track work — it never competes with it. If Berkeley's session was hard, this is the gentle counterweight, not extra grind.
  • Fuel and sleep are still the real rehab. The bone rebuilt on those; keep feeding it.
  • Loop your new PT and coaches in. Show them this — anything they want changed wins.
This is optional movement, not a training prescription or medical advice. It's built for a healing navicular that's out of the boot but still earning trust — self-directed work here stays gentle, non-impact, and pain-free, and defers every loading decision to Shaylan's PT and Berkeley staff. Stop anything that provokes the navicular spot and tell your care team.
