## THE GOVERNING MECHANICAL PRINCIPLE

Two lines from the June guide that should survive into every future decision:

> "It's a watershed-blood-supply bone, which means **pain-free is not the same as healed** — we protect it, we never test it."

> "The **tibialis posterior — the only tendon that attaches to the navicular** — pulls directly on it... **indirect tension can load a healing navicular even with zero weight through the foot.**"

Practical consequence: the restriction is not "no standing." It is **the right foot and ankle stay completely passive.** Anything that makes the foot or ankle do work — resisted plantarflexion, inversion, toe gripping, active arch holding, swim kick — tensions the navicular regardless of body weight.

This is also why **barefoot** loading is diagnostically significant. Barefoot walking on hard floors is the highest tibialis-posterior-demand activity in ordinary daily life: no midsole to blunt loading rate, active arch control every step, full push-off through the medial column.

## TRAINING PROGRAM BUILT (boot phase, v1.3)

Four sessions/week, all NWB, plus a daily core/glute anchor.

- **Day 1** upper push + core (seated/supported)
- **Day 2** NWB conditioning — arm ergometer intervals + core circuit
- **Day 3** upper pull + core (seated/chest-supported)
- **Day 4** upper hypertrophy + seated/lying mobility
- **Daily** single-leg bridge + ISO, modified side plank + hip abduction, dead bugs
- **Leg tier 3x/wk** (cleared in writing) — left-leg heavy eccentric as the cross-education driver; right leg open-chain only, foot dead-passive

**Cross-education rationale:** training the uninjured limb yields roughly 10-15% of the trained side's strength gain in the untrained limb via shared spinal/cortical wiring; immobilised-limb studies show reduced strength and muscle loss and faster post-immobilisation recovery (Farthing 2009; Carr 2025). Dosed per expert consensus (Manca 2021): 3x/week heavy eccentric-biased work, 4-6 weeks. Mirror at midline as an optional booster.

**Deliberate exclusions:** right calf / heel raises (plantarflexion pulls the navicular), aqua-jog (foot push-off), any swim kick.

**Cleared adjuncts:** band + open-chain hip series; swimming with pull buoy **plus** ankle strap so kicking is physically impossible; right-quad NMES at true contraction intensity (Dirks 2014); BFR for the immobilised thigh, cuffs already owned, pressure/rep scheme deferred to PT (Zhao 2020; Hughes review).

## OPEN CLINICAL QUESTION (unresolved since June)

The June guide flagged this for the six-week reexam and **the record does not show it was ever answered:**

> "Is clearance purely clinical (pain-free), or do you want CT/MRI to confirm bony union first, given it's a high-risk navicular?"

At week 12, with persistent symptoms on a watershed bone, this is the decision that matters. **CT, not MRI** — marrow oedema on MRI can persist for months after a navicular has united, so MRI cannot answer the union question here.

Secondary, cheaper hypothesis worth testing first: seven weeks of immobilisation atrophies the intrinsic foot musculature and calf. Diffuse barefoot ache across the arch and forefoot is consistent with deconditioning; **focal tenderness at the N-spot** (dorsal proximal navicular) is not. Palpation discriminates. **Do not use hop testing on a high-risk site.**

Third option, low cost: supportive footwear with a medial post indoors instead of barefoot, to reduce tibialis posterior tension directly rather than immobilising the whole foot again.
